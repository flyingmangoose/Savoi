# COMMON QUESTIONS

**We already know the technical language of our products.**  
That’s exactly why this works. The knowledge currently lives in a few people’s heads and they get interrupted constantly. The agent becomes the always-on version of your best technical expert so the humans only handle the high-value conversations.

**Why not just use ChatGPT?**  
ChatGPT is a brilliant intern with amnesia. No permanent memory of your products, no access to your systems, no ability to take action, and no consistent brand voice. Every conversation starts from zero.  
What we install is a specialist employee that already knows your catalog, your language, and can actually do things.

**What LLMs do you use?**  
Multi-model architecture on purpose. Complex reasoning and brand-sensitive work runs on the strongest models. Routine and high-volume work runs on faster, more cost-efficient models. Better quality where it matters and controlled cost. The system is model-agnostic.

**Doesn’t Salesforce Agentforce / Microsoft Copilot already do this?**  
Those platforms are excellent inside their own ecosystem. Most real workflows jump across Slack or Teams, email, CRM, inventory, quoting tools, and tribal knowledge. Platform agents stay constrained by their parent system.  
We build the agent that sits above the tools and owns the process end-to-end. Many clients already run Copilot or Agentforce and still need this layer.

**How do you prevent hallucinations or wrong answers?**  
Two controls. First, the agent answers from the approved knowledge base and tools we give it. Second, we put human approval gates on anything high-stakes (pricing, commitments, technical claims). Routine educational questions can run fully autonomously. You set the boundary.

**This seems expensive / We could build it ourselves.**  
You can. Most companies that try end up with a fragile prototype that works in a demo and falls apart in production. You’re buying a production system with observability, cost controls, reliable escalation, and an architecture that doesn’t collapse when the product catalog or team changes. The internal cost of doing it properly is usually higher once you count opportunity cost.
