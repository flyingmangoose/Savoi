## Platform

web

## Users
Primary: operations, technology, and sales leaders at mid-to-large B2B companies — especially manufacturers and distributors — who are evaluating whether to trust AI with real revenue-generating processes. They are pragmatic, cost-conscious, and have been burned by chatbot demos. They sit in offices, review proposals, manage teams, and integrate tools (CRM, Slack/Teams, email, inventory systems).

Secondary: technical buyers (CTO/CIO, heads of engineering) who need to understand architecture, security, and control before approving.

## Product Purpose
Savoi installs production multi-agent AI systems that live inside a client's tools, own real workflows, and operate with persistent memory, tool access, and human approval gates. It also builds focused micro SaaS products extracted from proven agent patterns. Success means a client's senior people get their time back because a digital employee owns a process end-to-end.

## Positioning
AI employees that own real work — not chatbots. The differentiator is production-grade systems with coordinated sub-agents, persistent memory, tool integrations, model routing for cost/quality control, and human-in-the-loop gates on anything high-stakes. Built on technology proven in high-stakes government delivery. No competitor in the generic AI chatbot space offers coordinated, deployed, managed agent systems with this level of operational control.

## Operating Context
Buyers evaluate through a 30-minute workflow mapping call, then a 2–3 week discovery/architecture phase, then a 3–6 week build/deploy phase. The product lives inside the client's existing tool stack: CRM, email, Slack/Teams, calendars, internal APIs, databases, inventory systems. Engagement can include ongoing managed service. Pricing ranges from $25k–$90k implementation plus $2.5k–$10k/month managed service.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- Core agent + sub-agents (orchestrator plus specialized agents for Sales, Ops, Support, Finance, Knowledge Work)
- Persistent memory and company knowledge
- Tool use and integrations (CRM, email, Slack/Teams, calendars, APIs, databases, inventory)
- Model routing (strong models for complex/brand-sensitive work, efficient models for routine/high-volume)
- Cron and autonomous jobs (scheduled workflows, monitoring, proactive alerts)
- Human-in-the-loop gates (pricing, commitments, liability, judgment require human approval)
- Multi-model, model-agnostic architecture
- Micro SaaS products: RFP/Proposal Engine, Revenue Guardian, Ops Sentinel, Meeting→Action, Industrial Sales Agent (most are planned/emerging, not yet launched)

## Product Principles
1. Production systems, not demos — everything must convey operational maturity and reliability.
2. Human-in-the-loop is a feature, not a limitation — the buyer sets the boundary.
3. Proven before productized — micro SaaS only emerges from patterns that worked in real installations.
4. Own the process — agents are employees with memory, tools, and accountability, not chatbots.
5. Multi-model by design — quality where it matters, cost control everywhere else.
